# SafeFlow facilitator quick-start guide

For a colleague running their ward's first SafeFlow practice session. Simulation-only — nothing in SafeFlow is a real patient, a real record, or a real clinical decision. Say that out loud at the start of every session.

## Before the session

- Pick 3-4 scenarios from the Scenario Library, not all fifteen. Recommended first-session set:
  - One of the original discovery scenarios with the clearest success signals (a good "this is what a well-run review looks like" opener).
  - `scenario-respiratory-rate-first` or `scenario-premet-worried-criterion` — evidence-grounded, and the "worried" criterion tends to spark good discussion because it validates instinct alongside numbers.
  - `scenario-family-concern-marthas-rule` — reliably the most engaging scenario to discuss as a group, because most staff have a story about a family member who was right.
  - Optionally one of the newer reflective panels (bias awareness, PACE ladder, Safety-II) if the group has 30 minutes rather than 20.
- Open the live demo URL on a shared screen if possible; SafeFlow works fine projected or on a laptop passed around.
- Have `international-comparative-synthesis.md` or `evidence-base-references.md` open in a second tab if anyone asks "where does this come from" — every scenario has an evidence citation built into the card.

## Suggested script (20-30 minutes)

1. **Open (2 min).** "This is a simulation tool, not a real system — there's no real patient data, and it doesn't do anything automatically. It's here to practise structured review and escalation thinking."
2. **Walk one scenario together (5 min).** Read the ward context and review prompt aloud. Ask the group what they'd want to know before making a call, before revealing the success signals and hazards.
3. **Let people drive (10 min).** Hand over the screen or let people use their own device. Encourage them to try 2-3 scenarios at their own pace.
4. **Discuss (5-10 min).** What felt familiar? What felt different from how the ward actually works? Any of the reflective panels (bias awareness, PACE ladder, Safety-II, PEARLS debrief) worth a closer look?
5. **Close.** Remind the group this is a discussion aid, not a replacement for local escalation policy, professional judgement, or existing NHS pathways like Martha's Rule.

## Capturing feedback (informal)

There's no built-in feedback form yet. If something confused people or a scenario didn't land, a simple note is enough: what scenario, what was confusing, what you'd change. Pass it back to whoever's coordinating the SafeFlow rollout on your patch.

## Common questions

- **"Is this replacing NEWS2 / our EWS?"** No — the score-comparison panel exists specifically to show the same observations under two different published scoring conventions; it's a teaching tool, not a scoring engine for real use.
- **"Does it store anything about our ward?"** No. Nothing is entered, saved, or transmitted from the reflective panels; the ward/trust data in the Trust Network and longitudinal views is fictional, built for the demo.
- **"Can I use this for real handover?"** No — SafeFlow is simulation-only and should never be used for real clinical decisions or real patient information.
