# SafeFlow — Evidence Horizon Scan: Overlooked Literatures, Interviews and Speeches

Status: research-support document for the SafeFlow simulation-only prototype. Fictional patients only.
Not for clinical use. Human review required.

Purpose: the comparative synthesis (`international-comparative-synthesis.md`) mined the clinical and
nursing-education literature. This scan deliberately looks OUTSIDE that lane — at policy, human
factors, aviation, organisational psychology, patient-voice campaigns, debriefing science, and named
interviews/speeches/documentaries — to find source families we had not initially considered, and to
map each to a concrete SafeFlow use. Items marked **[verified 2026]** were checked against live
sources during this scan; all others are well-established works that should be verified against the
original before formal citation in the demo pack.

## 1. Patient- and family-activated escalation — the biggest missed vein **[verified 2026]**

**Martha's Rule (NHS England).** Named for Martha Mills, who died of sepsis in 2021 after her
family's concerns were not acted on. Now a national programme: phase 2 (from Aug 2025) extends it to
all acute inpatient sites in England, with full implementation expected during 2026/27. Live NHS
England programme data (Sept 2024 - Apr 2026): **14,654 calls; 72% raised via the family/carer
route; 4,632 related to acute deterioration; 2,720 led to treatment changes; 616 led to escalations
of care** (HDU/ICU, enhanced care, specialist transfer). A 2026 Frontiers in Health Services
evaluation found **93% of surveyed patients/relatives were unaware** the service existed even in a
mature, well-advertised system — awareness, not appetite, is the barrier.
Related lineage worth citing: **Call 4 Concern** (Mandy Odell, Royal Berkshire — the UK nurse-led
precursor), **Ryan's Rule** (Queensland, named for Ryan Saunders), **Condition H(elp)** (Josie King
case, Pittsburgh). Interviews/first-person sources: **Merope Mills** (Martha's mother) — her Guardian
essays and broadcast interviews are the definitive first-person account of hierarchy failure from
the family's side.
**SafeFlow use (high priority, directly buildable):** a "family concern / Martha's Rule pathway"
discovery scenario — the board must surface a family-raised concern as a legitimate, visible
escalation trigger alongside staff concern (extends the SF-260 pre-MET worried-criterion scenario to
the patient/family voice), plus an awareness cue reflecting the 93%-unaware finding. This also
future-proofs SafeFlow against the strongest current NHS policy signal in exactly its domain.

## 2. Human factors and crew resource management — where graded assertiveness comes from

- **Martin Bromiley** — airline captain whose wife Elaine died in a routine anaesthetic procedure
  (2005); founded the Clinical Human Factors Group. The re-enactment film **"Just a Routine
  Operation"** (~14 min) and his many talks/interviews are the single most powerful teaching asset on
  hierarchy, fixation and speaking up in healthcare. Directly aligned with SafeFlow's speak-up
  scenario (SF-262).
- **Aviation CRM lineage** — Tenerife 1977 and United 173 (1978) are the origin cases for crew
  resource management; the **PACE** model (Probe, Alert, Challenge, Emergency) is the canonical
  graded-assertiveness ladder and a ready-made structure for SafeFlow's speak-up script.
- **Rhona Flin** — *Safety at the Sharp End*; the **ANTS** (Anaesthetists' Non-Technical Skills)
  taxonomy — the academic basis for teaching situation awareness/decision-making as skills.
- **James Reason** — Swiss-cheese model, *Human Error*, *Organizational Accidents*; frames SafeFlow's
  review cues as extra defensive layers, never a single barrier.
- **Charles Vincent** — *Safer Healthcare: Strategies for the Real World* (open access) — measurement
  and monitoring of safety; his "anticipation and preparedness" dimension matches SafeFlow's
  review-support framing almost word for word.

**SafeFlow use:** cite PACE explicitly in the graded-assertiveness scenario; link "Just a Routine
Operation" in the Learning Hub as recommended viewing; use Reason/Vincent language ("defences",
"anticipation and preparedness") in the demo-pack narrative to speak the safety community's dialect.

## 3. Psychological safety, just culture, and the second victim

- **Amy Edmondson** — *The Fearless Organization*; her research showing better-performing teams
  *report more* errors (they feel safe to). Her TEDx talk on psychological safety is short and
  presentation-ready. Grounds SafeFlow's non-punitive audit-trail framing.
- **Sidney Dekker** — *Just Culture: Balancing Safety and Accountability*; the restorative-vs-
  retributive distinction is the theory behind "audit trail framed for learning, not blame"
  (already in SafeFlow's hazard controls — now it has a citation).
- **Albert Wu** — "Medical error: the second victim" (BMJ 2000) — staff involved in incidents need
  support; motivates SafeFlow's non-punitive replay/debrief stance.

**SafeFlow use:** a one-line evidence footnote on the audit-trail/learning framing (Dekker,
Edmondson); a possible future "just-culture debrief" scenario where the review focuses on system
factors, not individual blame.

## 4. Safety-II and learning from what goes right

- **Erik Hollnagel** — Safety-I/Safety-II; the "From Safety-I to Safety-II" white paper. Most
  deterioration teaching studies failure; Safety-II says also study the many times wards catch
  deterioration well.
- **Learning from Excellence** (Adrian Plunkett, Birmingham Children's) — positive reporting
  (Ex-Cel/GREATix); a matching SafeFlow angle: scenarios can end with "what went right here" review
  cues, not only hazards.

**SafeFlow use:** add a "what went well" strand to scenario debrief cues — differentiating SafeFlow
from purely deficit-based training tools and aligning with the resilient-healthcare movement.

## 5. Foundational reports and speeches every NHS reviewer will recognise

- **Francis Report (Mid Staffordshire, 2013)** — unheeded deterioration and a culture that silenced
  concern; the moral case for SafeFlow's whole domain. Robert Francis's speeches/interviews on
  "freedom to speak up" extend it (and the national Freedom to Speak Up Guardian network exists
  because of it — a natural stakeholder audience for SafeFlow).
- **Berwick Report (2013), "A promise to learn - a commitment to act"** — "the NHS should become a
  learning organisation"; Don Berwick's IHI Forum keynotes ("Era 3" speech) argue for learning over
  measurement-and-blame — quotable framing for the demo pack.
- **NCEPOD "Time to Intervene?" (2012)** and the **RCP NEWS2 report (2017)** — the official clinical
  case for structured deterioration response; SafeFlow already teaches NEWS2, these are the
  authoritative anchors.
- **To Err is Human (IOM, 1999)** — the founding document of the field; one-line context citation.
- **Kate Granger — "#hellomynameis"** — her talks before her death in 2016; human introduction as a
  safety behaviour; a small but resonant touch for scenario scripts (introductions in SBAR openings).

## 6. Debriefing and simulation science — the pedagogy behind the product

- **Jenny Rudolph et al., "Debriefing with Good Judgment"** — advocacy-inquiry; the evidence-based way
  to run post-scenario review (vs judgmental or falsely neutral debriefs).
- **PEARLS framework** (Eppich & Cheng) — blended debriefing structure ready to template into
  SafeFlow's debrief cues.
- **INACSL Healthcare Simulation Standards of Best Practice** — the professional standard SafeFlow's
  scenario design can claim alignment with (design, debriefing, evaluation).
- **Roger Kneebone** — distributed simulation ("simulation doesn't need a sim centre") — supports
  SafeFlow's browser-based, low-cost positioning (pairs with Dwyer 2015 already cited).
- Journals to monitor: *Simulation in Healthcare*, *Advances in Simulation*, *BMJ Simulation &
  Technology Enhanced Learning* (now merged into BMJ STEL/Advances), *Clinical Simulation in Nursing*.

**SafeFlow use:** structure the scenario debrief text on PEARLS/advocacy-inquiry lines and say so —
"debrief cues follow published debriefing science" is a strong evaluator-facing claim.

## 7. Cognition and diagnostic error — why good nurses miss deterioration

- **Pat Croskerry** — cognitive dispositions to respond, dual-process theory in clinical reasoning,
  cognitive debiasing papers; anchoring/fixation/premature closure are exactly the failure modes
  SafeFlow's scenarios simulate (auto-filled RR accepted, cue lost in noise).
- **Daniel Kahneman** — *Thinking, Fast and Slow*; the accessible System 1/System 2 frame for the
  Learning Hub.
- **Checklist lineage** — **Atul Gawande** (*The Checklist Manifesto*; his 2014 BBC Reith Lectures
  "The Future of Medicine" are superb quotable speeches) and **Peter Pronovost** (CLABSI checklist)
  — structure catches what memory misses; SafeFlow's evidence-expected lists are mini-checklists.

**SafeFlow use:** name the biases in debrief cues ("this scenario rehearses anchoring/fixation") —
turns folk wisdom into taught metacognition, and gives the Learning Hub a reading/viewing list.

## 8. Workforce, migration and the internationally-educated nurse — the human bridge

- **WHO — State of the World's Nursing** reports; **ICN** policy briefs on nurse migration —
  macro-context for the Philippines/Malaysia workforce discussion in the synthesis (Theme 4 gap).
- **Transition literature**: studies of internationally-educated nurses' adaptation to UK practice
  (NMC OSCE preparation, escalation-culture adjustment; journals: *Journal of Transcultural
  Nursing*, *Nurse Education Today*, *Journal of Advanced Nursing*). This is the evidence base for
  the cross-cultural teaching-platform idea and partially fills the flagged Philippines gap.
- Search leads for the RCN library: "internationally educated nurses transition United Kingdom",
  "overseas nurses OSCE preparation", "Filipino nurses NHS adaptation escalation".

**SafeFlow use:** frames the cross-cultural platform pitch; a future scenario could rehearse the
first "I need to escalate" moment of a newly-arrived internationally-educated nurse — respectfully,
as a universal transition, per the synthesis's no-stereotyping rule.

## 9. Journals and standing sources to monitor (beyond those already used)

*BMJ Quality & Safety* (already central), *Journal of Patient Safety*, *International Journal for
Quality in Health Care*, *Applied Ergonomics / Ergonomics* (human factors), *Resuscitation* and
*Critical Care* (RRS/afferent-limb studies), *Journal of Clinical Nursing* / *JAN* / *Intensive &
Critical Care Nursing* (already productive via RCN), *Nursing Standard/RCNi* titles (practice
guidance), NHS England patient-safety publications and HSSIB (Healthcare Services Safety
Investigations Body) reports — HSSIB deterioration investigations are free, authoritative, and
scenario-ready.
