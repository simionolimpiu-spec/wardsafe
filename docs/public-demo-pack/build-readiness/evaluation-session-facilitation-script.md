# SafeFlow First Evaluation Session Facilitation Script

Status date: 2026-07-11  
Board item: SF-235 (docs-only)  
Session: first simulation evaluation, 3-6 nurses and/or nursing students, 60 minutes

This is the minute-by-minute, read-aloud script for the first evaluation session. Use it
with:

- `first-evaluation-session-plan.md` (SF-223) for the session objective, measures,
  thresholds and simulation boundary.
- `evaluation-feedback-capture-sheet.md` (SF-230) for live, per-task feedback capture.
- `evaluation-framework.md` (SF-220) for the anonymous pre-form, post-form, SUS items,
  data handling and reporting rules.

The session produces usability and perceived-usefulness evidence only. It is not a
clinical assessment, does not assess participants' practice, and must not be written up
as evidence of clinical benefit or patient-safety improvement.

## Before 00:00: facilitator setup

- Choose one fictional ward scenario and use the same scenario for every participant.
- Confirm that the build contains simulated data only. Do not enter names, real patient
  details, employer identifiers or other identifiable information.
- Prepare one participant code for each person: N1, N2, N3, and so on. Keep names off
  the capture sheet and out of the app.
- Prepare the evaluation framework pre-form and post-form, one copy of
  `evaluation-feedback-capture-sheet.md` (SF-230) per participant, a timer and a
  scribe's observation sheet.
- Test the exact path: fictional ward scenario -> review cues -> Ward Quality & Safety
  Review export -> one Competency Passport module.
- Agree who will facilitate and who will scribe. The facilitator keeps the prompts
  neutral; the scribe records pauses, navigation problems, help requests, quotes and
  wording concerns without judging whether a participant's interpretation is clinically
  right or wrong.

For a group session, give every consenting participant their own code and SF-230 sheet.
If participants share one screen, rotate the driver at each task and record who drove;
do not let other participants complete the task for the driver. If participants use
separate screens, start each task together and mark any assisted or interrupted run in
the notes. Do not compare participants during the session.

## 60-minute script

### 00:00-00:03 - Welcome and evaluation frame

Show the session agenda, but do not demonstrate the route through the product.

Say:

> "Thank you for taking part. Today we are evaluating how easy and useful this
> simulation-only prototype feels to use. The screens contain fictional, simulated data
> only. No patient, record or care activity is affected. This is not a test of you or
> your nursing knowledge, and there are no right answers to the feedback questions.
> Please tell me what you notice, where you hesitate and what you expect to happen.
> I will not coach you through a task unless there is a technical problem."

Then set the task order:

> "Everyone will follow the same three-step sequence: first, read the review cues;
> second, generate the Ward Quality & Safety Review export; third, complete one
> Competency Passport module. After each step I will pause for a short feedback entry.
> At the end I will ask about the overall experience."

If a participant asks whether an answer is correct, say:

> "There is no answer I need you to reach. I am interested in what the screen makes you
> think and what you would check next."

### 00:03-00:08 - Consent and participant codes

Read this once to the group, then confirm each person's answer privately or in the
agreed group format:

> "This is a simulation-only prototype. Every patient you see is fictional. No real
> patient data is used, and nothing you do affects a real person or record. I am asking
> for feedback on how usable and useful the tool feels. I will record only a participant
> code, such as N3, not your name or workplace. You can stop, skip a question or leave
> at any time, and you can ask me to discard your feedback afterwards. This session is
> about the prototype, not about assessing you. No clinical claims will be made from
> this session. May I use your anonymous feedback to
> improve SafeFlow and in anonymised summaries? Yes or no?"

Record the participant code, date and consent given (Y/N). Consent is for participants
only. There is no patient consent step because there are no patients and no patient data
in this session. Do not collect feedback from anyone who does not consent.

### 00:08-00:12 - Anonymous pre-form and handout of SF-230

First say:

> "Please complete the anonymous pre-session form from the evaluation framework using
> your own starting point. Use your participant code, not your name. Please answer
> independently; I will not interpret or correct your answers."

At 00:10, after consent and once the pre-form is underway, hand out one copy of
`evaluation-feedback-capture-sheet.md` (SF-230) to each consenting participant. Say:

> "This is the quick capture sheet we will use during the three tasks. Please complete
> the Session Details and Consent sections now, but leave the three task ratings blank.
> I will tell you when to rate each step. Use the free-text prompts for anything clear,
> unclear, useful, missing or slow. The sheet captures your experience; it is not a
> score of your performance."

Check that each sheet has a participant code and no name. Keep the sheet visible during
the walkthrough.

### 00:12-00:15 - Orient without demonstrating

Show the landing point only: the selected fictional ward scenario. Do not click through
the tasks. Say:

> "Here is the fictional ward scenario everyone will use. You may read the labels and
> instructions on screen. Please use the product as you would naturally use it. Think
> aloud as you work, including what you expect a button or heading to do. I may ask
> where you looked, but I will not lead you to an answer."

Start the timer for the task block. If someone needs a technical instruction, give the
smallest instruction necessary and mark the run as assisted.

### 00:15-00:23 - Task 1: read the review cues

Give the task exactly as follows:

> "Open the assigned fictional ward scenario. Read the Ward Safety Board and the
> Patient Journey Twin. Now read the review cues, including the explanation of why each
> cue was flagged. Talk me through what changed, what this cue is telling you and what
> you would check next. Please describe what you notice; do not try to make SafeFlow
> decide anything for you."

Use only these neutral probes if needed:

- "What makes you say that?"
- "Where did you find that on the screen?"
- "Did the explanation help you understand why it was flagged?"
- "What, if anything, were you expecting to see here?"

Do not say "correct", supply the missing interpretation or use nursing expertise to
steer the participant. The scribe records the first route taken, pauses, backtracking,
help requests, misunderstandings, exact quotes and any wording that feels like an
instruction rather than a review cue.

### 00:23-00:25 - Capture pause for review cues (SF-230)

Pause the screen and say:

> "Please pause here. On SF-230, rate the Reading review cues row from 1 to 5 based on
> how easy and useful that step felt. In the free-text box, note what made the cues clear
> or unclear and what slowed you down. Please also complete the Safety / Wording Note if
> anything sounded like an instruction, implied an action or was unclear. Do not change
> the rating after hearing anyone else's answer."

The scribe captures verbatim quotes before discussion moves on. If a participant says
the data felt real or authoritative, record the exact wording and flag it for the
hazard-log check after the session.

### 00:25-00:34 - Task 2: generate the Ward Quality & Safety Review export

Before the participant starts, say:

> "Now generate the Ward Quality & Safety Review export for the same fictional ward.
> Please find and generate it without me directing where to click. When it opens, read
> the summary back to us in your own words. This is a structured audit evidence export
> for simulation review, not a patient record."

When the export is visible, ask:

> "What is this helping you see? Is this the kind of summary you currently prepare by
> hand? What is missing, wrong or especially useful?"

Observe whether the participant can find the export, understands its sections, trusts
the boundary wording and can explain what the output is for. Do not ask whether the
export proves a real-world outcome. If the export cannot be generated, log the exact
error, stop the technical investigation and mark the task as interrupted rather than
quietly substituting a demonstration.

### 00:34-00:36 - Capture pause for the export (SF-230)

Say:

> "Please rate the Generating the Ward Quality & Safety Review export row on SF-230.
> In the free-text response, record what was missing, wrong or especially useful, and
> what would make this export easier to prepare or share. Add any wording or safety
> concern in the note below."

Record any participant request for a different heading, field, filter or export format
as a usefulness or usability observation, not as a product commitment.

### 00:36-00:44 - Task 3: complete one Competency Passport module

Give the task exactly as follows:

> "Open the Competency Passport learning area and complete one assigned micro-learning
> module. Work through it at your own pace and think aloud about the instructions,
> progress and knowledge check. When you finish, show whether the verified learning
> credit appears. Tell me what helped, what blocked you and what would make the module
> clearer. This is a test of the learning flow, not an assessment of your nursing
> competence."

If the participant asks for the answer to a knowledge-check item, say:

> "Please choose what you would choose without my help. I am observing the clarity of
> the module, not marking your answer."

The scribe records time to find the module, unclear instructions, navigation problems,
whether the completion state is visible and any request for help. Do not interpret the
learning result as evidence about the participant.

### 00:44-00:46 - Capture pause for the module (SF-230)

Say:

> "Please rate the Completing a Competency Passport module row on SF-230. In the free-
> text response, note what helped, what blocked you and what would make the module
> clearer. Add any wording concern or moment when the result felt overstated or unclear."

Check that each participant has completed all three SF-230 task rows before moving to
the post-form.

### 00:46-00:51 - Post-form and SUS

Put the product aside and hand out the anonymous post-form from
`evaluation-framework.md`. Say:

> "Please complete the evaluation framework post-form and SUS items using the same
> participant code. Answer independently, based on the whole session. There is no
> expected score and no need to justify an answer to me while you complete the form."

Do not explain the SUS scale or suggest a rating. The facilitator may clarify the
meaning of a form question by reading it again, but must not paraphrase it into a more
positive or negative claim.

### 00:51-00:58 - Structured debrief

Invite one response at a time and ask the same core prompts of each participant. Say:

> "I will ask a few short questions. Please speak from your own experience of the
> session. I am collecting usability and usefulness feedback, not judging your work."

Ask:

1. "What helped most when you were reading the ward picture and review cues?"
2. "What confused you or slowed you down?"
3. "Was the Ward Quality & Safety Review export the kind of summary you prepare by
   hand today? What was missing or wrong?"
4. "Did anything on screen feel like an instruction rather than a cue, or imply that
   SafeFlow was telling you what to do?"
5. "Was there any moment when you were unsure whether the data was real or simulated?"
6. "Would a tool like this help before the Expanse EPR go-live: yes, no or unsure, and
   why?"

Use the SF-230 Observer Notes for short quotes and the evaluation framework post-form
for the formal responses. If participants debate an answer, bring them back to their
own experience: "What did you personally see or expect at that moment?" Do not resolve
the debate during the session.

### 00:58-01:00 - Close and withdrawal reminder

Say:

> "Thank you. Your responses will be combined under participant codes and used only to
> improve this simulation-only prototype and describe its usability and perceived
> usefulness. We will not use this session to make claims about care, patient outcomes
> or clinical performance. If you want your anonymous feedback discarded, tell me now
> or contact me using the session arrangement."

Confirm that no one has left a name or real-world patient detail on a form, then stop
the recording or screen share if one was used.

## Neutral facilitation rules

- Keep the same scenario, task order and wording for all participants unless a
  technical interruption makes that impossible. Record any deviation.
- Ask what the participant expected, noticed or would check. Do not ask leading
  questions such as "Was that helpful?" before the participant has described the
  experience.
- Do not turn a review cue into an instruction, explain what a participant "should"
  have seen, or correct a participant's interpretation during the task.
- If a participant becomes uncomfortable, stop the task, offer a break and record only
  what they consent to retain.
- If a participant withdraws and asks for feedback to be discarded, mark the code as
  withdrawn and remove that participant's forms and notes from the session evidence.
- Treat a statement that data felt real, authoritative or action-directing as a safety
  observation. Capture the exact quote; do not explain it away.

## After the session

1. Check that each retained SF-230 sheet has a participant code, consent status, three
   task ratings, free text and any relevant Observer Notes. Keep raw forms private and
   offline; they do not go into SafeFlow.
2. Transfer only anonymised observations to the session results template in
   `first-evaluation-session-plan.md` (SF-223). Follow `evaluation-framework.md`
   (SF-220) for SUS scoring, pre/post measures, thresholds and the short report in
   `docs/evaluation-reports/`.
3. Create a hazard-log entry before the next session for any safety concern, any
   instruction-like wording, or any moment when simulated data was mistaken for real or
   authoritative information.
4. Report the result as usability and perceived usefulness evidence from 3-6
   participants in a simulation setting. Include the small sample, single-facilitator
   and simulation limitations. Do not infer clinical benefit, patient-safety impact or
   general performance from this session.
