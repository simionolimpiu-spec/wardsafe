# Public Case Corpus

**Status date:** 15 August 2026
**Protocol version:** 1.0
**Current size:** 2 included · 1 excluded (adjacent) — seed set only. Target: 100 included.

---

## What this is

A structured corpus of documented UK care trajectories in which a patient made **more than one contact** with a service about the same deterioration, **each contact was closed as appropriate**, the information that would have changed the outcome **already existed**, and **nobody re-opened the whole arc**.

Every case is drawn from a **published public record** — a coroner's Prevention of Future Deaths (Regulation 28) report, a PHSO published decision, or an HSSIB investigation. Every field is traceable to a quotable passage in that record.

## What this is not

**This is not patient data.** It contains no clinical records, no data obtained from any care provider, and nothing that is not already published by the judiciary, the Ombudsman, or a statutory investigator for the express purpose of public learning.

This distinction is load-bearing. `SafeFlow_Future_Discovery_IG_Checklist.md` establishes that everything in the WardSafe prototype uses fictional, simulated data, and that this is "the boundary that keeps SafeFlow shippable as a prototype." **This corpus does not cross that boundary.** It sits alongside `SafeFlow_Evidence_Base_References.md` and `safeflow-evidence-corpus-annotated-bibliography.md` as citable published evidence — not alongside `TwoTrust_patients.csv`.

Concretely:

| | Simulated patients (`TwoTrust_patients.csv`) | This corpus | Real patient records |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source | Invented | Published judicial/statutory record | Care provider |
| Identifies a real person | No | Yes — already published | Yes |
| UK GDPR applies | No | Not to the deceased¹ | Yes |
| DPIA / Caldicott route | No | No | Yes |
| Can be cited publicly | No | Yes | No |

¹ UK GDPR does not apply to the deceased. It **does** apply to living people incidentally identifiable from a report — bereaved family members, and named or describable staff. See *Handling* below.

## Why it exists

Two reasons, one schema.

**Ground truth.** `ml/generateSyntheticDataset.js` produces trajectories from assumptions. The risk engine currently asserts a pattern rather than having been validated against one. This corpus supplies documented instances of the failure modes the engine claims to detect, with the mechanism named by an independent judicial authority rather than by us.

**Evidence.** A claim that this pattern is real and recurring is worth very little unsupported. A claim backed by N adjudicated cases, drawn from a documented screening pool, with a coroner's own words attached to each, is a different kind of object in front of a Trust board, a commissioner, or an investor.

## The denominator matters more than the numerator

100 cases selected because they fit is an anecdote with a large sample size.

100 cases adjudicated out of a documented pool of N screened, with a reason code recorded for every rejection and an audited inter-rater disagreement rate, is evidence. The `excluded/` directory and `candidates.jsonl` are therefore **not** working files to be tidied away — they are the part of the corpus that makes the included cases mean anything. The first question a competent sceptic asks is "how many did you look at?"

## Method

1. **Harvest.** `node scripts/build-case-corpus.mjs --pages 250 --resume` crawls the ~2,500 reports in the judiciary.uk *Hospital Death (Clinical Procedures and medical management)* category, caches each page, and extracts the Reg 28 sections.
2. **Screen.** A crude signal lexicon gates candidates that show **both** a multiple-contact signal and a longitudinal-failure signal. This is a pre-filter for human attention, not a classifier.
3. **Adjudicate by hand.** Each gated candidate is read and assessed against C1–C4, with a verbatim quote recorded for each criterion. No case enters `cases/` without four quotes.
4. **Audit.** At least 20% of both included and excluded cases are independently re-adjudicated. The disagreement rate is recorded and published.

Step 3 is not automatable and should not be automated. A corpus whose inclusion decisions were made by a language model is not evidence — it is a claim about a language model.

## Handling

**Named deceased.** Cases are recorded under the names published by the coroner. This is what makes them citable and checkable; anonymising them would make the corpus unverifiable and therefore worthless as evidence. But the names belong to people whose families are living and who did not volunteer for this.

Accordingly:

- Never use a name in marketing, pitch, or promotional material. Cite the reference (`2026-0069`) in any commercial context.
- Never use a case as an illustrative anecdote where the reference is not also given, so a reader can go and read the coroner's actual words rather than ours.
- Never characterise a case beyond what the coroner found. The temptation to sharpen a narrative is exactly the thing that would discredit the corpus.
- Redactions in the source (`[REDACTED]`) are preserved verbatim. Do not attempt to resolve them.
- If a family asks for a case to be withdrawn, withdraw it and record the withdrawal in the manifest. The corpus loses one case; refusing would lose the corpus.

**Named staff.** Reports occasionally name or make identifiable individual clinicians. Living individuals — UK GDPR applies. Do not carry names of staff into case records. The unit of analysis is the system, and any case that only works as a story about one person's mistake has probably failed C4 anyway.

## Open questions

- **Survivorship.** PFD reports exist only where someone died *and* the coroner chose to report. The corpus therefore says nothing about frequency — only that the pattern recurs and is independently recognised. Any quantitative claim derived from it must state this. It is a corpus of confirmed instances, not a sample.
- **Repeat organisations.** Leary et al. (2021) found 36 reports raising repeat concerns with the same organisation. `authority.primary_organisation` is captured specifically so this can be tested within the corpus. If the same Trust appears repeatedly for the same longitudinal mechanism, that is the strongest single finding available here.
- **Weight tuning.** The harvester's signal weights are guesses. Once ~40 cases are adjudicated, measure the gate's precision against outcomes, retune, and record the change.
- **PHSO integration.** Not yet started. Different structure, anonymised, weaker clinical timelines but much better on what happened after the family complained — which is the second half of the story this corpus currently does not tell.
